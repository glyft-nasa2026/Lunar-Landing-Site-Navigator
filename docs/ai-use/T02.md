## What the tests found

Nothing wrong in the first pass. Worst differences from Skyfield over the 25 cases:

| | Altitude | Azimuth |
|---|---|---|
| Sun | 0.00069 degrees | 0.00002 degrees |
| Earth | 0.00010 degrees | 0.00020 degrees |

The Sun's 0.0007 degrees is about what the contract leaves out on purpose: the table gives the
Sun's direction from the Moon's centre, and standing 1,737 km to the side shifts it by
1737.4 / 149.6 million km = 0.00067 degrees. That is 30 times inside the tolerance.

At the south pole the Sun stayed between -1.586 and +1.578 degrees over the 1,000 times.

## Choices the AI made that the task doesn't spell out

## Known limits, not fixed here

| Limit | Importance | Why it stays |
|---|---|---|
| At exactly latitude -90 or 90 every direction is north (or south), so azimuths and `destination` bearings there are measured from the line of the longitude the site was given: bearing b from the south pole heads down longitude lon + b | *cosmetic* | The contract's `east` and `north` come from longitude. Altitude, which is what the pole is checked on, doesn't depend on it. `horizon.ts` uses the same rule |
| Earth's parallax for a site above the sphere is checked only by T00's made-up table, not by Skyfield | *cosmetic* | `altaz.json` has only elevation 0. Each kilometre of height moves Earth by at most 1 / 384,400 radians, 0.00015 degrees, so even the Moon's highest ground (about 10 km) moves it under 0.0015 degrees |
