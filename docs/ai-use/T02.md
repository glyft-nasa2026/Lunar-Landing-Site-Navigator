## What the tests found

Nothing wrong in the first pass. Worst differences from Skyfield over the 25 cases:

| | Altitude | Azimuth |
|---|---|---|
| Sun | 0.00069 degrees | 0.00002 degrees |
| Earth | 0.00010 degrees | 0.00020 degrees |

The Sun's 0.0007 degrees is about what the contract leaves out on purpose: the table gives the
Sun's direction from the Moon's centre, and standing 1,737 km to the side shifts it by
1737.4 / 149.6 million km = 0.00067 degrees. That is 30 times inside the tolerance.

At the south pole the Sun stayed between -1.586 and +1.578 degrees over the 1,000 times.

## Choices the AI made that the task doesn't spell out

P1 to confirm or overrule.

| Choice | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fixture cases run with ground 0 and `eye_m: 0` | `geometry.test.ts` | `make_ephemeris.py` builds the Skyfield point at `elevation_m=0.0` on the 1737.4 km sphere |
| Azimuth is compared only where the fixture altitude is below 89 degrees, as the task says. None of the 25 cases is that high (Earth's highest is 86.07), so every azimuth is compared | `geometry.test.ts` | Near the zenith a tiny change in direction swings the azimuth a long way |
| The 1,000 random times and the 500 `destination` cases come from a seeded generator (mulberry32), so a failure is the same on every run | `geometry.test.ts` | A failure that can't be repeated can't be fixed |
| T00's test "rises a little when the site is higher" is renamed "sits a little lower when the site is higher" | `geometry.test.ts` | The test already expected Earth lower from a higher site (a larger radius means more parallax); only the name was wrong |

## Known limits, not fixed here

| Limit | Importance | Why it stays |
|---|---|---|
| At exactly latitude -90 or 90 every direction is north (or south), so the azimuth there is measured from the line of the longitude the site was given | *cosmetic* | The contract's `east` and `north` come from longitude. Altitude, which is what the pole is checked on, doesn't depend on it |
| Earth's parallax for a site above the sphere is checked only by T00's made-up table, not by Skyfield | *cosmetic* | `altaz.json` has only elevation 0. Each kilometre of height moves Earth by at most 1 / 384,400 radians, 0.00015 degrees, so even the Moon's highest ground (about 10 km) moves it under 0.0015 degrees |
