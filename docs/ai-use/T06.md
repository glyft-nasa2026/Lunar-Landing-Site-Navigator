## Choices the AI made that the task doesn't spell out

P1 to confirm or overrule.

| Choice | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "A skyline of 30 degrees everywhere gives lit 0 %" is tested near the pole (latitude -88), not at the equator | `windows.test.ts`, `summary` | At the equator the Sun climbs overhead and clears a 30 degree skyline for the middle of the day: lit is 33.5 %, and a second test says so. Lit 0 % needs a Sun that stays low |
| "Within 1 minute" is measured against a one-second series, for Earth as well as the Sun | `windows.test.ts`, `nextFlip` | The halving stops under one minute, so the answer is the first time known to be on the far side of the flip: 0 to 57 seconds after it |
| The time `nextFlip` returns is one at which the body has already changed | `windows.ts`, kept from T00 | Asking again from that time gives the next flip, not the same one |
| Tests now hold T00's choices in place: `0 h 20 min` under one hour; a lone sample counts as one hour in `summary`; `series` leaves out its end time and times outside the table; a start before the table still finds the first flip inside it | `windows.test.ts` | They were choices in `docs/ai-use/T00.md` with no test behind them |

## Known limits, not fixed here

| Limit | Importance | Why it stays |
|---|---|---|
| `nextFlip` returns null both when nothing flips in 60 days and when the table ends first, so in the last 60 days of 2035 the readout can say "No sunset in the next 60 days" when the table has simply run out | *cosmetic* | Telling the two apart needs a change to the `Flip` type or the wording table, which are P1's contract |
| The hourly walk can step over a Sun that hides and comes back within one hour (a thin spike on the skyline) | *cosmetic* | The contract sets the hourly walk. The skyline is one value per degree and the Sun takes about two hours to cross a degree, so a real case is rare |
