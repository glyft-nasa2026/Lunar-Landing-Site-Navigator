## Why no coordinate is Bussey's

Bussey et al. (2010) worked on Kaguya topography and print their points to 0.01 degrees
(about 300 m). On our LOLA 240 m map all five of their points miss the crest they name: they are
lit 43 to 65 % of the time. The sunlit ground is small: on a 240 m grid within 2.4 km of points
B, D, C and M1, only 13, 4, 7 and 12 cells of 441 are lit 70 % of 2020 or more. So each point is
placed where a LOLA-based source puts the sunlit ground:

| id | Bussey's point, lit on our map (2020) | Now, and from where | Moved | Lit on our map (2020; 2026 to 2044) | Earth in view (2026 to 2044) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `shackleton-rim` | A, -89.68, -166.0: 46.7 % (whole table) | -89.6866, -162.81, Gläser S0 | 0.6 km | 84.0 %; 83.9 % | 48.0 % |
| `connecting-ridge` | B, -89.44, -141.8: 42.6 % | -89.4398, -137.17, Gläser C1 | 1.4 km | 90.4 %; 90.1 % | 53.5 % |
| `shackleton-ridge-120e` | D, -88.79, 124.5: 56.8 % | -88.8089, 123.76, NASA map | 0.7 km | 88.9 %; 88.4 % | 55.7 % |
| `de-gerlache-rim` | C, -88.71, -68.7: 64.6 % | -88.7061, -68.52, NASA map | 0.2 km | 77.8 %; 76.7 % | 51.6 % |
| `malapert-massif` | M1, -86.04, 2.7: 43.2 % | -86.0412, 3.16, NASA map | 1.0 km | 77.8 %; 76.8 % | 53.8 % |

- **Every percentage in this table is our project's calculation**, not a figure from a paper:
  this repo's `core/` code (`skylineAt` and `series` on `dem.bin` and the 1900-2100 table,
  hourly, eye height 2 m), counting an hour as lit when any of the Sun's disc shows.
- **NASA's map agrees that Bussey's points are off the crest.** It reads 37 %, 43 %, 50 % and
  19 % at points B, D, C and M1, and 87.1 % at `shackleton-rim`, where its brightest pixel
  within 3 km is the one the point sits in.
- **The three "NASA map" points are not named in a study.** They are a rule applied to NASA's
  published map: the brightest 60 m pixel within 1 km of Bussey's point. The file was read over
  HTTP, only the tiles around each point; the script stayed in the session scratchpad.
- Bussey's yearly percentages (82 %, 86 %, 85 %, 74 % of 2020) belong to their own points on
  their own map, so `sites.json` does not quote them.
- **Don't put a point back on a paper's rounded coordinate** without running it through the
  engine: `web/src/core/sites.test.ts` fails when one of these five is lit 70 % of 2027 or less
  on the real map.

Bussey prints Point D's latitude three ways: 88.74 S in the abstract and the conclusions,
88.79 S in one place, "99.79" in the body. Neither 88.74 nor 88.79 is on the crest of our map
(60.4 % and 56.8 % lit in 2020).
